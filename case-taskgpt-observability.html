Observability

TaskGPT is the AI suite I designed at TaskUs for agents on live customer calls, and for the people who set up, measure and improve the AI behind them. I was the only designer, from 2022 to 2025. Observability is one of its 11 products.

See every AI interaction step by step, and judge if the answer was acceptable.

The story

Our SVP proposed it, after the quarter-end question that shaped the design (see "One real moment" in Part 1). It became TaskUs's first actionable instrumentation of AI tools on client campaigns, built on two principles: data minimisation and privacy by design. The idea was to follow each query through every step: sensitive-information detection and masking, small talk, input guardrails, file reading, the vector database, the model call and output guardrails, until the answer reaches the user. For each step it records the time taken, and if a step fails, where and why. People can debug down to the raw data from the front end. My job was to make heavy, complex data easy to read, understand and fix.

What I designed

  • Campaign view. Total active users and total provisioned users this month, each with its change and a definition you can open ("What is Active Users?"). A table of campaigns shows active users, provisioned users and an active-user trend line. Export to CSV.
  • Interaction logs. One row per query: user, time, query, feedback (thumbs up or down), the response, and a status flag.
  • The status flag. A bar of lettered steps for the AI pipeline: chatbot triggered, incoming guardrails, embedding call, vector database call, sent to the model, outgoing guardrails, PII masking and request status. The letter shows where a request stopped. Hover over it to see the overall time, the step that failed, and how the time splits between the middleware and the AI. Open the row to see each step with its timestamp, time taken and a remark such as "Request failed".
  • Interaction detail. Tabs for response, prompt, metadata, LLM, feedback and citation. Metadata shows the masking flag, response time, database and model times, and tokens. LLM shows the engine, model, embedding model and BCP mode. Citation shows the file, document ID, version, chunk and score.
  • Review. "Is the response acceptable?" Yes or No. A No asks for the reason: knowledge gap, guardrails or knowledge base. The view shows who reviewed it last, and Previous and Next move through the queue. Log review also shows the original text beside a translation.
  • Smaller layouts at 1280 px wide for review on laptops.

Design decisions

  • Each step has a letter, so a person can name the failed step in a ticket. Colour alone cannot do that.
  • Complex data in tabs: response, prompt, metadata, LLM, feedback and citation. Each person opens only what they need.
  • The three review reasons match the review chart on the dashboard, so every review counts in the numbers.
  • After a fix in the back end, the reviewer runs the query again from the prompt tab to check it.
