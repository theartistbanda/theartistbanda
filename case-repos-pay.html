Repos Pay: diesel on credit, with trust on both sides

About 9 minutes to read

Repos Energy delivers diesel to the doorstep of businesses through mobile petrol pumps. Many of these businesses buy fuel in large amounts, but their money comes in on long cycles. So they need credit. For the pump owner who sells the fuel, credit is a risk: bad debt, paperwork and legal steps.

Repos Pay is the credit service I designed from scratch to close that gap. A customer orders fuel, says how much credit they need and for how many days, and signs a digital agreement in the same flow. The pump owner sees the request and signs to accept it. Repos checks the customer's CIBIL score and runs a background check through a third-party firm. On that basis, Repos guarantees the transaction.

In the same project, I revamped the main Repos delivery app and built Repos Pay into it. The work covers five surfaces: the customer web dashboard, the customer app, the partner web dashboard, the partner app, and the Repos admin portal. The revamped app and Repos Pay both went live.

The brief

Repos Energy is a Pune company. It builds mobile petrol pumps and works with fuel station owners to deliver diesel to the doorstep. Its customers are businesses. The sample data in the files shows housing societies, industrial sites and hotels.

These customers buy fuel often and in large amounts. But their money comes in on long cycles, so paying for every order up front is hard. Credit is the obvious answer. For the pump owner, it is also a risk. A customer who does not pay becomes bad debt. Every credit deal needs paperwork and a legal agreement.

Repos Pay had to give the customer credit, and give the pump owner enough trust to say yes.

The project had two parts. I revamped the main Repos delivery app, for customers and for partners. And I designed Repos Pay from scratch and built it into that app, so credit became part of the normal way to order fuel.

Three users, one deal

Every Repos Pay order has three parties. Each one needs something different.

  • The customer needs fuel today and time to pay. They want to know the full cost before they agree.
  • The pump owner (partner) sells the fuel. They need to know who they lend to, what is due, and when.
  • Repos (admin) stands in the middle. It needs to see every agreement, every payment and every problem in one place.

So I designed one deal seen from three sides. The same order, the same agreement and the same due date appear on every surface. Only the actions change.

Research

Before I designed a screen, I spoke with the people on each side of a credit deal:

  • A few petrol pump owners. They would give the credit, so they carry the risk.
  • Decision makers at companies that order fuel. They would ask for the credit and sign for it.
  • Some creditors who work in the fuel industry. They already lend in this market, so they know how credit goes wrong.

These talks became the three users of the product.

The biggest finding was about the apps they already used. Most of them knew only WhatsApp and Dukaan (an Indian app that lets small sellers set up an online shop from a phone). Both need very little technical knowledge. So simple use mattered more than visual detail. I kept every screen free of clutter: one question at a time, plain words, and only the numbers needed for the next decision.

Trust came from checks the customer never sees. Repos verifies each customer's CIBIL score and runs a background check through a third-party firm. That is why Repos can guarantee the transaction, and why a pump owner can say yes to a customer they do not know.

Key design decisions

1. Credit is a choice inside the normal order

A customer does not go to a different product to buy on credit. The order form has a Repos Pay option. In the app, the customer sees one question: "Do you want to use ReposPay service?" If they say no, the normal order continues. The Figma file maps both paths side by side: a normal order, and an order from an active Repos Pay user.

On the pump list, a "PayLater Only" switch shows only the pumps that accept Repos Pay. Each of those pumps carries the Repos Pay mark, next to stock, rating, rate, fuel density and time of arrival. The customer can sort by cheapest or fastest.

2. Ask two questions: how much, and how long

The credit step asks for the credit amount. Next to it, the customer sees the order total and the amount they still pay now. Then they choose a period: 3, 7, 15 or 30 days. Each option shows the estimated amount payable. In the app, it is one plain question: "In how many days will you pay back?", with the estimated payable value above it.

3. The full cost comes before the signature

The order summary shows each part of the price on its own line: fuel amount, credit days, credit amount, convenience charge, GST and total. A side panel shows the credit terms: credit days, credit amount, score, interest and incentive. The customer can view and download the agreement PDF before they sign. If the price for the delivery date is not set yet, the screen says so, and says that the bill will use the price on the day of delivery.

4. A signature that works on any device

Customers and pump owners sign in three ways: draw, type, or upload a photo of their signature (JPEG or PNG, up to 1 MB). The "Tutorial" page in the customer app file shows the signature box rebuilt as one interactive component with three states, in place of three separate screens.

5. Both sides sign

The order does not start until the pump owner agrees. On the partner web and app, a credit request arrives as a card under "Pending Decision" with Accept and Reject. The order detail shows what the pump owner needs to decide: the part payment, the credit amount, the credit days and the due date. Then the pump owner signs the same agreement and accepts the order.

6. A score the customer can improve

Credit terms depend on the Energy Score. The onboarding FAQ explains it in one line: an average of the customer's CIBIL score, social proof and payment history. The dashboard shows the score on a gauge, with a label ("Good Score") and how much it moved last week and last month. A good score means lower interest, and paying before the due date earns incentives. Small lines remind the customer at the right moment: "Delayed payments will affect Energy Score." The score in the order summary comes with a "Tips to Improve" link.

7. Onboarding that shows where you are

Before a customer can use credit, they upload KYC documents. The onboarding screen shows five steps in "How it works": find Repos Pay pumps, enter the credit amount, select the period, sign the agreement, done. Next to it, "Get started" shows each document with its status. A rejected document shows the reason and an upload button, so the customer knows what to fix.

8. Offline payments still count

Some customers pay by cheque or demand draft. Online payment opens Razorpay, and offline payment is a choice on the same screen. On the dashboard, one banner asks: "Have you paid offline? Upload your source of truth." The customer or the pump owner uploads the proof. In the admin portal, each proof appears in a "Payment Source of Truth" table with who uploaded it, and Repos can accept or reject it.

9. What is owed, always in view

The customer home shows three counts: ongoing, overdue and due orders, each in its own colour. Each order card has a bar that fills as the due date comes near, with "Due in 3 days" or "Today" in red. Cards that are due carry Call and Reminder actions. Notifications say it in plain words: "You have 1 payment pending today."

10. One place for Repos to hold the risk

The admin portal sorts every agreement into Pending, Accepted, Expired and Issue. An agreement with an issue says what went wrong, in one line: "Customer was expected to make payment on 17-10-2020." The agreement page shows the credit details, the fee split (2% to the partner and 1% to Repos in the sample), the incentive and penalty rates with a "How is it calculated?" link, and the twelve sections of the agreement as rows that open on demand.

For each pump owner, Repos can switch Repos Pay on or off, set the revenue share and the operating distance, and check KYC documents, credit given and payment received. For each customer, Repos sees a credit profile in one card: Energy Score, CIBIL score, Karza score, social proof rating, payment history score, the last three payment methods, type of entity, MSME class and sector. Repos can also set a custom weightage for the Finserv Pay Check (FPC) for one customer.

11. Trust shown, not only promised

"Secured with Blockchain" sits under every signature and every order confirmation. In the admin portal, each agreement has a blockchain link next to view and download. Every transaction was planned to go on a blockchain, as part of the company's future plans.

12. A brand inside a brand

Repos Pay needed its own identity, but it had to sit inside the Repos apps. I designed the Repos Pay brand. I kept the Repos purple for the parent brand and gave Repos Pay its own blue, with a full set of blue and purple tones in the style sheet. The Repos Pay mark appears on every pump, order and card that uses credit, so the customer can see at a glance which orders are on credit.

13. A moment of delight at the end

Placing an order ends with a short animation, not a plain message. On the web, it is a Lottie animation. In the app, it plays under "Order Placed", and the pump owner sees the same moment as "Order Accepted".

From Finserv to Repos Pay

The "Old Alteration Versions" page shows where the product started. It was first called Repos Finserv, with its own logo, and the score was the Diesel Score. The old onboarding explained two journeys: three steps to get a score (upload your documents, get your Diesel Score, done), and five steps to order. A "Credit History with Repos Pay" card showed dues, payments on time, incentive and interest rate.

In the later version, the name became Repos Pay and the score became the Energy Score. The onboarding keeps the five order steps, and the document steps became a "Get started" list with a status on each document.

The rename was a business decision. At the time, many companies were launching their own wallets, such as Amazon Pay. "Repos Pay" put the product in that family, and it gave the name room to grow with the company's future plans.

The partner's day

Repos Pay sits inside the partner's daily work. The partner app has a small daily task: "Update Diesel & Density for Today", with the rate and the density, and one "Set" button. Then the Repos Pay dashboard shows total credit given, total payment received and the number of Repos Pay orders. Filters let the partner jump to pending decisions, Repos Pay orders or orders to deliver. I designed the full partner app as part of the revamp, including profile, business details, settings and notifications.

Outcome

The revamped Repos delivery app and Repos Pay both went live. Customers could order diesel on credit from the app or the web. Pump owners could accept or reject each credit request and follow every payment. Repos managed every agreement, payment proof and issue from one portal.

What I learned

Learn the industry before the screens. I learned how the fuel business works from the inside: its thin margins, and how credit really works in it.

Credit checks are part of the product. I learned how services like CIBIL and Experian are used inside a product. I also learned to work with digital KYC products and design them in our own style, so the checks felt like part of Repos.

Many stakeholders, one product. Repos was my first project across many cross-functional domains. I worked with several stakeholders at the same time.
