Context

A national moment needed a national visual identity.

In 2020, Prime Minister Modi announced Aatmnirbhar Bharat (Self-Reliant India), a sweeping economic response to the COVID-19 crisis. The initiative needed a symbol: something that could represent Make in India, self-sufficiency, and national unity simultaneously.

MyGov opened a public competition for the mark, and I entered.

The design

Three ideas, one mark.

The challenge was precise: a mark that says self-reliance without feeling isolationist, and unity without being generic. It had to work everywhere from a phone screen to factory packaging.

01 / Colour

The tricolour

Saffron, white and green, the colours of the national flag.

02 / Centre

The Ashok Chakra

The wheel at the heart of the flag, anchoring the mark.

03 / Story

The golden bird

A nod to India once being called sone ki chidiya, the golden bird.

04 / Build

One proportional grid

Every curve and angle built on a single system, so it holds up from a phone screen to packaging.

Adoption

Taken up by PM SVANidhi.
Used on Indian-made goods.

The competition was never formally decided. The mark was taken up anyway by PM SVANidhi, a micro-credit scheme for street vendors and one of the most visible COVID-19 relief programmes in India. From there, manufacturers began using it to mark Indian-made goods.

None of it came with credit. Today a large number of manufacturers across India use the mark on their packaging.

No credit also means no easy way to prove reach. What exists instead is the trail it left: the messages from strangers who found it and used it, unprompted.
