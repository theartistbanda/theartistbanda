The knowledge assistant agents use on live calls. It answers from the client's own knowledge base.

The story

AssistAI is the first TaskGPT chatbot, the one at the start of this case study. When TaskGPT became the name of the whole suite, the chatbot became AssistAI. It is the fully equipped chatbot agents use on calls: each client's details at their fingertips, answered through RAG from that client's own knowledge base.

What I designed

  • A chat that shows its source of truth. The header shows the client and the knowledge base version, for example "Ver. 15". When the knowledge base changes during a chat, a divider says so: "KB updated to Ver. 15.0.0".
  • Answer tools. A source tag (for example "Source: Web"), related questions, stats on demand (tokens and metadata), clear context, copy, regenerate, and thumbs up or down.
  • A playground switch, to test a bot before release.
  • Chat history with three visibility levels: private, default (you, your supervisor and your immediate reporter) and public. A change of chat type asks for confirmation, and says that the supervisor gets a notification.
  • Feedback in two layers. On each response (positive or negative, with a reason). On the product (an emoji rating, "Did you get the answers for your questions?", and which product they needed).
  • A floating version. A compact panel with a chatbot picker, a drawer for chat history, and file upload (the Chrome extensions are in section 12).
  • A sensitive-information check before a message goes out. The chat warns that the message looks like sensitive information, and offers three choices: Don't share, Share masked, or Yes (not recommended).
  • A welcome screen that sets expectations. It remembers the conversation, takes corrections and declines inappropriate requests. Error states for when the service is down. Light, dark and mobile layouts.

Design decisions

  • Show the version. Agents and reviewers can tie an answer to the knowledge it came from.
  • In the sensitive-information check, the safe choice comes first, and the risky choice says "Not recommended".
  • Help goes where the agent already is. A mid-call tab switch is a pause the customer hears.
