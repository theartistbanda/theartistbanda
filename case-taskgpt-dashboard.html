TaskGPT Dashboard

AI usage, quality and cost

Is the AI used, is it right, and what does it cost? Eight groups of KPIs, each with its definition. The screen where client trust formed, and the one that sold TaskGPT most.

TaskGPT is the AI suite I designed at TaskUs for agents on live customer calls, and for the people who set up, measure and improve the AI behind them. I was the only designer, from 2022 to 2025. TaskGPT Dashboard is one of its 11 products.

One page that tells a manager if the AI is used, if it is right, and what it costs.

The story

Like the Home Page, I proposed it, and everyone liked it. This is the screen where client trust formed, and the screen that sold TaskGPT most. It grew with the suite, as each new product added its numbers. The overview tracks users, sessions, responses and consumption. The feedback view tracks feature adoption, and averages each query's ratings for overall satisfaction, quality and coherence, and natural language understanding (NLU), with thumbs-up and thumbs-down details.

What I designed

  • Scope first. Product and instance at the top. Then filter chips for campaign, LOB and site, a designation filter (teammate, team lead, manager, operations manager) and a timeframe (today, yesterday, last 7 days, month to date, year to date or custom).
  • Six KPI groups in columns: users, engagement, responses, guardrails, sessions and consumption. Each card shows a ratio and a percentage, a trend line, and an arrow to the detail.
  • A definition for each KPI behind an info icon that shows on hover. For example: "Average tokens consumed per user in selected timeframe".
  • Tables under the KPIs: knowledge-gap queries, guardrail queries (input or output), valid queries and small-talk queries, each with a usage count.
  • A Feedbacks tab, and a review dashboard (March 2025). Feedback submitted, knowledge gaps, and thumbs up and down. Interactions, knowledge gaps and thumbs-down answers reviewed. A review disposition chart by reason (knowledge gap, guardrails, knowledge base and more).
  • Download and email the dashboard; empty and loading states; layouts at 1024 px and 1440 px wide.

What I instrumented

Eight groups of KPIs, each with a one-line definition in the product.

GroupKPIsThe question it answers
UsersActive users out of provisioned users, average active users, adoption at 50% or moreIs the AI reaching the people it was set up for?
EngagementUser engagement, average engagement, queries per user, interactions per userDo people come back, and how deeply do they use it?
ResponsesValid responses (valid intents out of all intents), knowledge gaps (partial understanding or small talk), failed responses (API or model failure)Is it answering correctly?
GuardrailsTotal, incoming and outgoing guardrail hitsIs it staying safe?
SessionsSessions and interactions, queries per session, average response timeHow do people use it, and how fast does it answer?
ConsumptionTotal tokens, tokens per user, tokens per sessionWhere is the oil going?
FeedbackFeedback submitted, thumbs up and down, average product, quality and NLU ratingsHow does it feel to use?
ReviewInteractions reviewed, knowledge gaps reviewed, thumbs-down answers reviewed, review reasonsWhat do we fix next?

Design decisions

  • Ratios over raw counts. A share of provisioned users tells a manager what to do.
  • Consumption sits beside quality. Token cost per user and per session shows where the oil is going.
  • Knowledge gaps show as a list of real queries, not only a number, so a knowledge owner can fix them in the KnowledgeBase Utility.
