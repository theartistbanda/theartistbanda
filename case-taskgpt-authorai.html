AuthorAI

AI writing assistant

One-click writing help, planned from a survey of how people used TaskGPT: summaries, tone, emails and action items. It also runs as a floating widget on almost any website, and inside Kustomer.

TaskGPT is the AI suite I designed at TaskUs for agents on live customer calls, and for the people who set up, measure and improve the AI behind them. I was the only designer, from 2022 to 2025. AuthorAI is one of its 11 products.

A writing assistant for agents and teams: draft, rewrite and summarise in the right tone. Its earlier name was TaskScribe.

The story

We wanted to go one step beyond chat. Writing a good prompt takes time, so we built one-click features on top of it. After TaskGPT launched, I ran a survey on how it helped agents and everyone else who used it. The patterns were clear:

  • Managers mostly summarised and drafted emails.
  • Team leads checked sentiment in conversations, and pulled action items from emails and documents.
  • Agents improved their writing and grammar, and polished their tone.

From these patterns I planned one-click templates: summarise (in bullets or paragraphs), sentiment analysis, change tone, improve writing, and drafts of emails, articles and announcements. Admins control access and see usage, so the features keep growing. The product got so much praise that we launched it on its own. When TaskUs planned to sell it commercially, the brand team named it AuthorAI (it started as TaskScribe). After that revamp, adoption across the renamed products rose by an average of 12%.

What I designed

  • A shortcut menu in the prompt bar. Write an email, a message, an article or a sentence; create an announcement; paraphrase; rephrase; templates; write a custom prompt.
  • Actions on text. Summarise, find action items, improve writing, fix spelling and grammar, change tone (professional, friendly or casual), sentiment analysis, and a pronunciation helper for one word.
  • Output layouts. Paragraph, bullet list or table.
  • Favourite templates, so common tasks take one click.
  • A privacy caution under the input. Do not share passwords or financial data, and the AI can make mistakes.
  • Article Generation. Upload a DOC, TXT or PDF (up to 10 MB), or a URL, and get an article back. A list shows each document as processing, finished or failed, with download and retry. Errors say what to do, for example "File size too big, maximum allowed 10mb" with Re-Upload.
  • A threaded view that puts AuthorAI content inside an AssistAI chat, plus light and dark themes.

Design decisions

  • Shortcuts start from the task, such as "write an email", not from a blank prompt, because writing a good prompt takes time.
  • The templates come from real use. Each one matches a pattern from the survey.
  • The September 2024 feedback round sits in the file, with screenshots of the live build beside the fixes.

AuthorAI also ran as a Chrome extension. Its floating widget works on almost any website or tool the agent uses, as long as the extension is on. It also has its own panel in the Kustomer agent workspace. Section 12 covers both.

TaskScribe and AuthorAI

A floating widget that works on almost any website or tool the agent uses, as long as the extension is on. The agent selects text, and a small toolbar appears beside it. From there they can summarise, explain, find action items, improve, fix grammar, make the text shorter or longer, simplify it, change its tone, or run it as a prompt. The toolbar opens a side panel with chat, templates (predefined and favourites), history and saved prompts. The file holds a feature spec with defaults: keep American or British English as found, write paragraphs, and keep subject lines and sentiment analysis off until asked. The May 2024 revamp became AuthorAI. The point of the revamps was to bring AuthorAI into the client's own systems, such as its own panel in the Kustomer agent workspace, so agents get its power where they already work. There, for a refund question, Find Action Items lists the refund link, email address and phone number to share.
